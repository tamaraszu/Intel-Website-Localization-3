Sustainability Through the Ages

Explore Intel's journey through time, discovering how our commitment to innovation has shaped a more sustainable future for technology and our planet.

1968

Intel Founded

Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore rename the newly formed company NM Electronics to Intel Corporation, laying the foundation for decades of technological innovation.

1971

First Microprocessor

Intel debuts the 4004, the world's first commercial microprocessor, igniting the microprocessor revolution and propelling the future of computing devices.

1978

8086 Processor

Launch of the 8086 processor, establishing the x86 architecture that drives countless PCs and servers in the modern era.

1985

386 Processor

Intel introduces the 386 processor with 32-bit architecture, ushering in a new era of performance and multitasking for personal computers.

2006

Peak GHG Emissions

This year marks Intel's highest annual greenhouse gas emissions for operations. Over subsequent years, Intel invests heavily in chemical abatement, renewable energy, and energy-efficient manufacturing to reverse this trend.

2020

RISE Strategy

Intel launches its RISE (Responsible, Inclusive, Sustainable, Enabling) strategy and 2030 goals, aiming to drive industry-wide progress on climate action, water stewardship, and waste reduction.

2022

Net-Zero By 2040

Intel announces its commitment to achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions (Scope 1 and 2) across its global operations by 2040, building on years of environmental initiatives.

2023

Renewable Electricity

The company achieves 99% renewable electricity usage worldwide, helping to drastically lower carbon emissions and driving progress toward Intel's long-term sustainability goals.

2024

Sustainability Summit

Intel hosts its first Sustainability Summit, uniting suppliers, government officials, and industry leaders to collaborate on next-generation sustainable semiconductor manufacturing.

Sustainability Highlights

🌱 Environment

Intel has reduced its greenhouse gas emissions by over 60% since its 2006 peak, driven by chemical abatement programs and renewable energy investments worldwide.

💧 Water

Through its 2030 RISE goals, Intel aims to achieve net positive water use — restoring more fresh water than it consumes across all of its global manufacturing sites.

♻️ Waste

Intel targets zero total waste to landfill and upcycles manufacturing byproducts, diverting thousands of tonnes annually away from disposal and into reuse streams.

Sustainability Deep Dive

Intel has reduced its absolute greenhouse gas emissions by over 60% since its 2006 peak. This has been achieved through large-scale chemical abatement systems installed at manufacturing facilities, a shift to renewable electricity sources, and continuous improvements in energy efficiency across chipmaking processes. Intel's net-zero commitment targets Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions by 2040, with interim milestones reviewed annually.

Semiconductor manufacturing is water-intensive, requiring ultra-pure water for chip fabrication. Intel's 2030 RISE goals include achieving net positive water use — meaning Intel will restore more fresh water to local watersheds than it consumes. This involves water recycling programmes, conservation projects, and partnerships with local communities near manufacturing sites in the US, Israel, Ireland, and Malaysia.

Intel targets zero total waste to landfill across its global operations. Manufacturing byproducts such as solvents, acids, and metals are recovered and diverted into reuse or recycling streams wherever possible. Intel also works with suppliers to reduce packaging waste and is exploring closed-loop material recovery for rare elements used in chip production.

In 2023, Intel achieved 99% renewable electricity usage across its worldwide operations. This was accomplished through a combination of directly sourced renewable energy contracts, renewable energy certificates (RECs), and on-site solar installations. Intel works with energy providers in each country where it operates to match consumption with clean generation, adapting to the constraints of local energy grids.

Launched in 2020, Intel's RISE strategy stands for Responsible, Inclusive, Sustainable, and Enabling. The 2030 goals span four pillars: achieving net positive water use, reaching zero total waste to landfill, maintaining over 90% renewable electricity, and driving supplier adoption of sustainable practices. Progress is publicly reported each year in Intel's Corporate Responsibility Report.
